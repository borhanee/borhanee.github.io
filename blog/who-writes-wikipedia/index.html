Who Writes Wikipedia?

Published: July 27, 2026 4 min read Borhan Uddin

When people think about Wikipedia, one question often comes to mind that "Who writes all these articles?"

The answer is surprisingly simple: people from around the world. Wikipedia is not written by a company, a government, or a team of paid editors. Instead, it is built and maintained by millions of volunteers who contribute their time and knowledge.

A Global Community

Since its launch in 2001, Wikipedia has grown into the world's largest free online encyclopedia. Its contributors include teachers, students, engineers, doctors, historians, librarians, journalists, and people from countless other backgrounds.

Anyone can edit most Wikipedia articles. Some people make a single correction, while others spend years improving articles, reviewing edits, or helping new contributors.

These behind-the-scenes efforts help keep Wikipedia accurate and up to date.

Reliability Comes from Sources

A common misconception is that Wikipedia relies on the expertise of individual editors. In reality, it relies on reliable published sources.

Editors are expected to support their contributions with books, academic journals, newspapers, and other trustworthy references. Personal opinions or unpublished research are not accepted.

Wikipedia's content is guided by three core principles:

  • Verifiability: Information must be backed by reliable sources.
  • Neutral Point of View: Articles should present topics fairly and without bias.
  • No Original Research: Editors cannot publish their own ideas or findings.

Because every edit is publicly recorded, other contributors can review and improve the content over time.

Who Runs Wikipedia?

Many people assume the Wikimedia Foundation writes Wikipedia. It does not.

The Wikimedia Foundation is a nonprofit organization that provides the technical infrastructure, fundraising, and legal support needed to keep Wikipedia online. Editorial decisions are made by the volunteer community through discussion and consensus.

Some experienced volunteers become administrators, but they are not editors-in-chief. Their additional permissions help them fight vandalism and maintain the site—they do not control what Wikipedia says.

A Living Encyclopedia

Unlike traditional encyclopedias, Wikipedia is constantly evolving. Articles are updated whenever new information becomes available, whether it's the result of a scientific discovery, a major news event, or newly published research.

This collaborative editing process has enabled Wikipedia to grow into one of the largest collections of freely accessible knowledge ever created.

Final Thoughts

So, who writes Wikipedia?

The answer is everyone who chooses to contribute responsibly. Behind every article is a community of volunteers working together to improve the quality of free knowledge.

Wikipedia's greatest strength is not that every editor is an expert. Its strength lies in transparent collaboration, reliable sourcing, and a shared commitment to making knowledge freely available to everyone.
